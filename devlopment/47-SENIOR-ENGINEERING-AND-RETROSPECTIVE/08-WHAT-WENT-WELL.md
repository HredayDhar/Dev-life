# 08 — What Went Well

## 1. What Is This?

"What Went Well" is a reflective practice focused on identifying and acknowledging successes, positive outcomes, and effective practices from a project, sprint, or time period. Rather than dwelling on problems or shortcomings, this approach intentionally looks for what worked effectively, what delivered value, and what practices contributed to positive results. By recognizing and understanding successes, teams can reinforce effective behaviors, build on strengths, and create a more balanced perspective that supports continuous improvement.

## 2. Why Does It Matter

Focusing on what went well directly impacts:
- **Morale and Motivation**: Recognition of success boosts team spirit and engagement
- **Strength-Based Improvement**: Building on what works is often more effective than fixing what's broken
- **Best Practice Identification**: Reveals effective techniques worth standardizing and sharing
- **Confidence Building**: Reinforces belief in the team's capabilities and effectiveness
- **Positive Psychology**: Encourages optimism and resilience in facing future challenges
- **Learning Balance**: Prevents overly negative focus that can obscure genuine progress
- **Replication Potential**: Identifies patterns that can be intentionally repeated
- **Stakeholder Communication**: Provides positive evidence to share with sponsors and leadership
- **Risk Mitigation Understanding**: Shows what preventive measures or practices were effective
- **Team Cohesion**: Shared recognition strengthens bonds and shared purpose
- **Innovation Encouragement**: When successes are noticed, teams feel safer to experiment
- **Retention Support**: Feeling effective and valued contributes to job satisfaction
- **Process Validation**: Confirms that certain approaches, tools, or methodologies work
- **Individual Growth**: Helps team members recognize their own contributions and strengths
- **Celebration Culture**: Creates moments to acknowledge effort and achievement

## 3. Conducting Effective "What Went Well" Reflections

### Setting the Stage
- **Create Safety**: Emphasize that this isn't about arrogance but learning from success
- **Time Allocation**: Dedicate sufficient time (often 15-30 minutes in retrospectives)
- **Environment**: Ensure comfortable space for reflection and sharing
- **Materials**: Provide sticky notes, digital tools, or other capture methods
- **Facilitation**: Guide the process to avoid superficial or repetitive responses

### Individual Reflection
- **Silent Writing**: Begin with quiet individual thinking before group sharing
- **Prompt Questions**: Use specific questions to stimulate thoughtful responses
- **Timeboxing**: Limit individual reflection to maintain energy and focus
- **Inclusivity**: Ensure everyone has opportunity to contribute
- **Documentation**: Capture thoughts privately before group discussion

### Group Sharing
- **Round Robin**: Often works well to ensure everyone speaks
- **Clarification**: Ask for specifics when responses are vague
- **Building**: Allow team members to expand on each other's points
- **Grouping**: Combine similar items to identify patterns
- **Validation**: Acknowledge each contribution without judgment
- **Visualization**: Use boards or screens to make contributions visible

### Analysis and Synthesis
- **Pattern Recognition**: Look for common themes across multiple contributions
- **Root Cause Exploration**: Why did these things go well? What enabled them?
- **Context Consideration**: What specific circumstances contributed to success?
- **Transfer Potential**: Which successes could work in other contexts?
- **Resource Identification**: What people, tools, or practices enabled these outcomes?
- **Timing Factors**: When during the period did things go particularly well?

### Documentation and Follow-up
- **Capture Insights**: Record not just what went well, but why it worked
- **Share Widely**: Consider sharing successes beyond the immediate team
- **Create Action Items**: Identify how to replicate or extend successful practices
- **Update Practices**: Modify standards, checklists, or guidelines based on learning
- **Recognize Contributors**: Acknowledge specific individuals or pairs when appropriate
- **Build Knowledge Base**: Add to team's collection of effective practices

## 5. Common Challenges and How to Address Them

### Difficulty Identifying Successes
- **Problem**: Team struggles to name specific things that went well
- **Solutions**:
  - Start with very small, specific observations
  - Review metrics, deliverables, or completed work items
  - Ask about moments of pride or satisfaction
  - Consider what complaints *didn't* happen
  - Look for avoided problems or disasters prevented
  - Use timelines to jog memory about the period
  - Compare against initial goals or expectations

### Superficial or Generic Responses
- **Problem**: Responses like "communication was good" without specifics
- **Solutions**:
  - Ask for concrete examples or instances
  - Request details about who, what, when, and how
  - Probe for impact: "How did that specifically help?"
  - Use the "Five Whys" technique on positive statements
  - Ask what would have happened if it hadn't gone well
  - Request evidence or observations that support the claim
  - Encourage naming specific people, practices, or events

### Discomfort with Self-Praise
- **Problem**: Team members feel awkward acknowledging success
- **Solutions**:
  - Frame as learning, not bragging: "What can we learn from this?"
  - Emphasize team over individual achievements
  - Focus on systemic factors rather than personal traits
  - Normalize that recognizing success is professional, not arrogant
  - Start with appreciating others before self-recognition
  - Share that even experts need to recognize what works
  - Connect to future benefit: "Understanding this helps us repeat it"

### Dominance by Certain Perspectives
- **Problem**: Same people or viewpoints dominate the discussion
- **Solutions**:
  - Use structured techniques like round-robin or silent writing first
  - Directly invite quieter members to share
  - Acknowledge contributions and then ask others: "What about you?"
  - Use anonymous collection methods initially
  - Split into small groups for initial discussion
  - Establish and remind about airtime expectations
  - Have facilitator actively manage participation balance

### Focusing Only on Outcomes, Not Process
- **Problem**: Only celebrating end results, ignoring how they were achieved
- **Solutions**:
  - Ask specifically about practices, not just outcomes
  - Probe for the "how" behind every "what"
  - Consider efficiency, learning, or team health as outcomes
  - Recognize effort and adaptation, not just success
  - Ask what was learned during the process, not just the result
  - Highlight effective responses to setbacks or challenges
  - Value experimentation and iteration, even if final outcome varied

### Failure to Connect to Action
- **Problem**: Nice discussion but no change in behavior afterward
- **Solutions**:
  - Always ask: "How can we do more of this?"
  - Identify specific practices to adopt or amplify
  - Create experiments to test transferring successes to new contexts
  - Update checklists, definitions of done, or team agreements
  - Assign owners to propagate successful practices
  - Schedule follow-up to check on replication efforts
  - Share successes with other teams who might benefit

## 6. Integrating "What Went Well" into Regular Practice

### In Retrospectives
- **Dedicated Time**: Allocate specific portion for positives
- **Balanced Approach**: Pair with improvement discussions
- **Varied Formats**: Sometimes lead with positives, sometimes end with them
- **Consistent Capture**: Always document what went well alongside improvements
- **Trend Tracking**: Monitor whether noted successes increase over time
- **Action Connection**: Link positive findings to specific experiments

### Beyond Retrospectives
- **Daily Standups**: Brief appreciations or recognition moments
- **Kanban Boards**: "Done" column celebrations or notes
- **Code Reviews**: Explicitly note what's done well, not just what needs fixing
- **Deployment Celebrations**: Acknowledge successful releases
- **Milestone Recognition**: Mark significant achievements explicitly
- **Learning Sessions**: Share success stories in brown bags or tech talks
- **Onboarding Materials**: Include examples of effective practices
- **Performance Reviews**: Incorporate identified strengths and successes
- **Hiring Interviews**: Discuss what success looks like in the team
- **Client Reports**: Share what went well as part of progress updates

### Organizational Practices
- **Knowledge Repositories**: Tag and organize success patterns
- **Communities of Practice**: Share what's working across teams
- **Leadership Communication**: Executives sharing what they observe working
- **Award Systems**: Recognize teams or individuals for effective practices
- **Promotion Criteria**: Include ability to identify and build on strengths
- **Training Programs**: Build curriculum around proven successful practices
- **Organizational Myths**: Create and share founding success stories
- **Best Practice Extraction**: Systematically turn observations into guidelines

## 7. Scaling "What Went Well" Across Contexts

### For Individuals
- **Personal Retrospectives**: Regular individual reflection on successes
- **Skill Journals**: Document what worked in learning or skill application
- **Strength Spotting**: Regularly identify personal contributions that worked
- **Feedback Loops**: Seek specific positive feedback from others
- **Energy Tracking**: Note activities that left you energized rather than drained
- **Experimentation Logs**: Record what personal experiments succeeded
- **Mentoring Moments**: Identify times when helping others worked well
- **Career Reflections**: Periodically assess what aspects of work feel fulfilling

### For Distributed Teams
- **Async Collection**: Allow time-zone-flexible input before synchronous meeting
- **Digital Tools**: Use shared documents, boards, or apps for capture
- **Video Sharing**: Enable seeing facial expressions when sharing positives
- **Structured Turn-Taking**: Explicitly manage who shares when in virtual settings
- **Celebration Adaptation**: Find remote-appropriate ways to mark successes
- **Care Packages**: Send physical tokens of appreciation when possible
- **Virtual Coffee**: Schedule informal time just to connect positively
- **Follow-up Messages**: Send appreciation notes after positive observations

### For Cross-Functional Groups
- **Language Translation**: Ensure terms are understood across specialties
- **Perspective Validation**: Acknowledge that "well" might mean different things
- **Impact Translation**: Connect successes to each group's goals or metrics
- **Joint Recognition**: Celebrate achievements that required multiple functions
- **Dependency Appreciation**: Specifically note when other teams helped
- **Shared Metrics**: Develop combined views of what constituted success
- **Rotation Hosting**: Alternate which function leads the reflection
- **Common Vocabulary**: Build shared language for discussing effectiveness

### For Leadership Teams
- **Strategic Outcomes**: Focus on organizational, not just team-level, successes
- **Systemic Recognition**: Note when policies, structures, or processes worked
- **Stakeholder Feedback**: Incorporate external perspectives on what worked
- **Resource Allocation Review**: Did investments produce expected returns?
- **Capability Building**: What leadership development efforts proved effective?
- **Culture Indicators**: What behaviors suggest desired culture is emerging?
- **Innovation Tracking**: What experiments or initiatives showed promise?
- **Risk Management**: What preventive measures successfully mitigated issues?
- **Succession Progress**: What talent development or readiness improved?
- **Decision Quality**: What choices led to particularly good outcomes?

## 8. Measuring the Impact of Focusing on What Went Well

### Leading Indicators
- **Frequency of Recognition**: How often team members acknowledge positives
- **Specificity of Comments**: Move from vague to concrete, detailed observations
- **Participation Breadth**: Percentage of team regularly contributing positives
- **Action Follow-through**: Percentage of "do more of this" attempts actually tried
- **Best Practice Adoption**: Speed at which noted successes spread to others
- **Meeting Sentiment**: Observed energy and engagement during positive discussions
- **Psychological Safety Signals**: Comfort sharing both struggles and successes
- **Learning Artifacts**: Documents, presentations, or other capture of success insights

### Lagging Indicators
- **Retention Correlation**: Teams practicing recognition show better retention
- **Innovation Metrics**: Increased experimentation when strengths acknowledged
- **Performance Trends**: Improvements in velocity, quality, or predictability
- **Customer Satisfaction**: Correlation with positive internal recognition patterns
- **Employee Engagement Scores**: Particularly questions about recognition and growth
- **Promotion Rates**: Internal advancement when strengths are visible and valued
- **Knowledge Sharing**: Increased documentation or teaching of effective practices
- **Conflict Reduction**: More constructive disagreements when positivity baseline exists
- **Attendance and Punctuality**: Reliability improves when work feels rewarding
- **Psychological Health Metrics**: Lower burnout, higher resilience measurements

### Qualitative Evidence
- **Narrative Stories**: Specific examples of how recognizing success changed behavior
- **Behavioral Shifts**: Noticeable increases in helping, sharing, or experimenting
- **Language Changes**: Team discourse includes more appreciative framing
- **New Rituals**: Organic emergence of recognition practices in daily work
- **Cross-Team Invitations**: Other teams seeking to learn from observed successes
- **Leadership Noticing**: Leaders commenting on positive team dynamics
- **Troubleshooting Shift**: More solution-oriented rather than blame-oriented responses
- **Meeting Transformation**: Less dread, more anticipation of reflective time
- **Identity Evolution**: Team begins seeing itself as capable and effective

- **Process Victories**: What working methods served the project well?
- **Relationship Building**: What stakeholder or client connections strengthened?
- **Technical Achievements**: What engineering feats were particularly proud of?
- **Team Growth**: How did individuals develop or capabilities expand?

## 10. Conclusion

Consciously identifying what went well is not about creating an artificially positive narrative or avoiding necessary criticism. Instead, it's about creating a complete picture that includes both challenges and successes, problems and solutions. When teams regularly practice recognizing and understanding their successes, they develop a more accurate sense of their capabilities, build genuine confidence based on evidence, and create a foundation for sustainable improvement that builds on strengths rather than constantly fighting weaknesses. The most effective teams and organizations don't just fix what's broken—they also amplify what works. By making "What Went Well" a regular, intentional practice rather than an occasional afterthought, teams create positive feedback loops where recognizing success leads to more success, ultimately building more capable, resilient, and fulfilled engineering teams. In the relentless pursuit of better outcomes, taking time to acknowledge and learn from what already works is not just morale-boosting—it's strategically essential.
