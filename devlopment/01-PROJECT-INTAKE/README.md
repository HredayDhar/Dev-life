# Phase 01 — PROJECT-INTAKE

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of the Project Intake phase is to formally initiate a software project by capturing the initial idea, understanding its alignment with business goals, and establishing the foundational information needed to proceed with further analysis and planning. This phase ensures that only worthwhile ideas receive investment of time and resources, and that stakeholders have a shared understanding of what is being proposed.

## 2. What This Phase Is

Project Intake is the gateway process where ideas for new software projects or significant enhancements are collected, logged, and given an initial review. It involves capturing high-level information about the proposed project, such as its objectives, potential value, rough scope, and initial stakeholder identification. This phase does not involve detailed analysis but rather screens ideas to determine which deserve further investigation.

## 3. Why This Phase Exists

Organizations generate many ideas for software projects, but limited resources mean not all can be pursued. Project Intake exists to:
- Prevent wasted effort on low-value or infeasible ideas
- Provide a consistent method for logging and tracking project proposals
- Ensure early alignment with strategic goals and available capacity
- Create a transparent process for stakeholders to submit and track ideas
- Gather sufficient information to make an informed decision about whether to proceed to detailed problem definition

## 4. Where It Fits in the Software Development Lifecycle

Project Intake occurs at the very beginning of the software development lifecycle, before any significant investment in analysis or design. It feeds into the Problem Definition phase (02) by providing approved project concepts that need deeper exploration. In lifecycle models, this corresponds to the "ideation" or "conceptualization" stage.

## 5. When This Phase Starts

This phase starts when:
- An individual or group identifies a potential software solution to a problem or opportunity
- A formal request for a new project is submitted through an established channel
- Leadership initiates a strategic initiative that requires software development
- Feedback from users, customers, or operations suggests a need for a new system or enhancement

## 6. What Must Be Known Before Starting

Before initiating Project Intake, it is helpful to know:
- The organization's strategic goals and current priorities
- Any established intake processes, forms, or tools
- Who is responsible for managing the intake process (e.g., PMO, product management)
- What criteria will be used to evaluate ideas (though these may be refined during intake)
- The typical timeline and effort expected for intake activities

## 7. Inputs

Inputs to the Project Intake phase include:
- Raw ideas from stakeholders (customers, users, executives, developers, etc.)
- Market research, competitor analysis, or trend reports
- Feedback from support tickets, user surveys, or analytics
- Strategic planning documents outlining business objectives
- Capacity and resource availability information
- Any existing project request forms or templates

## 8. Activities

Typical activities in Project Intake include:
1. Receiving and logging project ideas through a standardized process (form, meeting, email, etc.)
2. Capturing essential information: project name, description, proposer, date submitted
3. Conducting a preliminary assessment of alignment with business goals
4. Estimating very rough scale (small, medium, large) or effort (if possible)
5. Identifying apparent stakeholders who would be affected or interested
6. Noting any known constraints, dependencies, or risks mentioned in the submission
7. Performing an initial triage: accept for further review, reject with feedback, or request more information
8. Communicating the decision and next steps to the idea proposer
9. Adding accepted ideas to a backlog or queue for the Problem Definition phase

## 9. Outputs / Deliverables

The primary outputs of Project Intake are:
- **Project Intake Record**: A document or entry in a tracking system containing the captured information
- **Initial Assessment**: A brief note on alignment with goals, rough value estimate, and recommendation
- **Updated Project Backlog**: A list of approved ideas awaiting detailed analysis
- **Feedback to Proponents**: Communication to idea submitters about the status of their suggestion
- **Metrics**: Data on number of ideas received, accepted, rejected, and cycle times (for process improvement)

## 10. Who Is Involved

- **Idea Proponents**: Anyone who submits a project idea (users, customers, employees, stakeholders)
- **Intake Coordinator**: Person or team responsible for managing the intake process (often PMO, product management, or a designated analyst)
- **Reviewers**: Individuals who perform the initial assessment (may include product managers, architects, finance representatives)
- **Stakeholder Representatives**: Those who may be consulted to understand impact or feasibility (occasionally)
- **Leadership/Portfolio Managers**: Those who ultimately decide which ideas receive further investment (though detailed decisions may come later)

## 11. Step-by-Step Workflow

1. **Idea Submission**: A stakeholder submits a project idea via the established intake channel (form, meeting, etc.)
2. **Logging**: The intake coordinator records the idea in the tracking system with basic details (ID, title, description, proposer, date)
3. **Initial Review**: The coordinator performs a quick check for completeness and clarity, requesting clarification if needed
4. **Preliminary Assessment**: Reviewers evaluate the idea against strategic goals, rough value, and obvious feasibility
5. **Decision**: Based on the assessment, the idea is:
   - **Accepted**: Moves to the Problem Definition phase for deeper analysis
   - **Rejected**: Declined with specific feedback provided to the proposer
   - **On Hold**: Deferred due to timing, resource constraints, or need for more information
6. **Notification**: The proposer is informed of the decision and any next steps or feedback
7. **Tracking Update**: The idea's status is updated in the tracking system, and accepted ideas are added to the backlog for the next phase
8. **Process Improvement**: Periodically, intake metrics are reviewed to identify bottlenecks or areas for streamlining

## 12. Real-World Example

**Scenario**: A retail company wants to improve its online presence.

**Project Intake Process**:
1. **Submission**: A store manager submits an idea via the company's internal portal: "Develop a mobile app for customers to browse inventory and make purchases from their local store."
2. **Logging**: The PMO logs the idea with ID PROJ-2026-087, title "Local Store Mobile App", description as submitted, proposer name, and date.
3. **Initial Review**: The coordinator checks the submission is complete and asks the manager to clarify whether this is for a specific pilot chain or all stores.
4. **Preliminary Assessment**: The product management team reviews:
   - Alignment with goal to increase digital sales (strong alignment)
   - Rough estimate of potential revenue increase based on similar apps (moderate-high)
   - No obvious showstoppers (requires mobile dev skills, which the team has)
   - Notes dependency on accurate real-time inventory data (to be investigated)
5. **Decision**: The idea is accepted for further problem definition, with a note to inventory system readiness as a key question.
6. **Notification**: The store manager receives an email: "Your idea has been accepted for initial analysis. A product owner will contact you within 5 days to discuss details."
7. **Tracking Update**: PROJ-2026-087 moves from "Intake" to "In Analysis" in the project tracking system.
8. **Later**: After problem definition and feasibility study, the project may proceed to detailed planning or be rejected if inventory data cannot be made available in time.

## 13. Junior Developer Perspective

As a junior developer, you might encounter Project Intake indirectly:
- You may be asked to provide a rough effort estimate for an idea during intake
- You might hear about new projects during team meetings and wonder where they came from
- You could submit your own ideas for improving tools or processes through the intake channel
- You might see the intake tracker or dashboard showing what projects are being considered
- Your role is to provide realistic input when consulted and to understand that not all ideas become active projects

## 14. Senior Developer Perspective

Senior developers engage with Project Intake more strategically:
- They may serve as reviewers to assess technical feasibility and risks
- They help shape the intake criteria to ensure technical considerations are weighed early
- They use intake data to anticipate future work and plan team capacity or skill development
- They may propose ideas themselves based on technical debt, emerging technologies, or system limitations observed in maintenance
- They understand that a good intake process prevents wasted engineering effort on poorly conceived projects
- They recognize that intake is where early technical questions (like "Do we have the data?" or "Can we integrate with X?") should be raised

## 15. Common Mistakes

- **Vague Submissions**: Accepting ideas with insufficient detail to make any meaningful assessment
- **No Clear Criteria**: Making accept/reject decisions based on opinion rather than defined objectives
- **Black Hole Process**: Ideas disappear into the intake process with no feedback to proposers
- **Overburdening Intake**: Requiring too much detail upfront, discouraging submissions
- **Ignoring Capacity**: Accepting more ideas than can be realistically analyzed, creating a large backlog
- **Lack of Tracking**: No visibility into what ideas have been submitted, accepted, or rejected
- **Skipping Intake**: Allowing projects to start without any formal vetting, leading to poorly aligned work
- **Only Top-Down**: Ignoring ideas from frontline employees or customers who see problems directly

## 16. Risks

- **Missing Good Ideas**: Poorly advertised or complex intake process discourages valuable suggestions
- **Wasted Analysis Time**: Accepting ideas that are clearly misaligned with strategy or infeasible
- **Delay in Feedback**: Proposers disengage if they don't hear back promptly
- **Analysis Paralysis**: Spending too much time on intake, delaying actual work on worthy projects
- **Bias Toward Novelty**: Favoring exciting new ideas over important but mundane maintenance or technical debt projects
- **Inadequate Stakeholder Input**: Missing critical perspectives because only certain groups are consulted
- **Tool Overhead**: Using cumbersome tools that make the intake process itself burdensome

## 17. Security Considerations

- **Idea Submission**: Ensure the intake mechanism does not introduce security vulnerabilities (e.g., injection attacks via forms)
- **Data Protection**: Protect any sensitive information submitted in ideas (e.g., customer data, proprietary concepts)
- **Confidentiality**: Handle ideas that may involve confidential or proprietary information appropriately
- **Access Control**: Restrict who can submit ideas if needed (e.g., internal-only vs public-facing)
- **Audit Trail**: Maintain logs of who submitted what and when for accountability and intellectual property tracking
- **Early Threat Detection**: Use intake as an opportunity to raise initial security questions about proposed projects

## 18. Performance Considerations

- **Process Efficiency**: The intake process itself should be lightweight to avoid becoming a bottleneck
- **Tool Performance**: Ensure intake tracking tools are responsive and don't deter usage
- **Batching**: Consider whether ideas should be reviewed individually or in batches for efficiency
- **Automation**: Use automation for routing, acknowledgments, and status updates where appropriate
- **Scalability**: The process should scale with the number of idea submitters without degradation
- **Metrics Tracking**: Collect lightweight metrics on intake volume and cycle times to identify performance issues

## 19. Scalability Considerations

- **Volume Handling**: Design the intake process to handle everything from a few ideas per month to hundreds
- **Team Scaling**: As the organization grows, consider decentralizing intake (e.g., by business unit) with central coordination
- **Tool Scaling**: Ensure intake tracking systems can handle increasing data volume and user load
- **Process Standardization**: Maintain consistency in how ideas are evaluated across different parts of the organization
- **Integration**: Consider how intake feeds into downstream project management tools and processes
- **Resource Planning**: Use intake data to inform hiring, training, or capacity planning decisions

## 20. Quality Considerations

- **Clarity of Submission**: High-quality intake captures clear, actionable ideas rather than vague notions
- **Consistency**: Apply the same evaluation criteria to all ideas to ensure fairness
- **Completeness**: Gather sufficient information to make a reasonable go/no-go decision without overburdening the proposer
- **Feedback Quality**: Provide specific, actionable feedback to proposers, whether accepted or rejected
- **Process Adherence**: Ensure the intake process is followed consistently rather than bypassed for convenience
- **Data Quality**: Maintain accurate and complete records in the intake tracking system
- **Continuous Improvement**: Regularly review intake effectiveness and refine the process based on outcomes

## 21. Definition of Done

The Project Intake phase is complete when:
- [ ] The idea has been logged with essential identifiers and description
- [ ] Basic information has been captured (proposer, date, contact information)
- [ ] An initial assessment of alignment with strategic goals has been performed
- [ ] A decision has been made to accept for further analysis, reject, or defer
- [ ] The proposer has been notified of the decision and any feedback or next steps
- [ ] The idea's status has been updated in the tracking system
- [ ] Accepted ideas have been added to the backlog for the Problem Definition phase

## 22. Completion Checklist

- [ ] Standard submission method is available and known to potential proposers
- [ ] Intake coordinator or team is designated and trained
- [ ] Basic intake form or template captures necessary information
- [ ] Preliminary assessment criteria are defined (even if high-level)
- [ ] Notification process for proposers is established
- [ ] Tracking system (spreadsheet, tool, etc.) is set up and maintained
- [ ] Rejected ideas receive constructive feedback
- [ ] Accepted ideas flow smoothly into the next phase
- [ ] Intake metrics (volume, cycle time, acceptance rate) are tracked
- [ ] Process is reviewed periodically for improvements
